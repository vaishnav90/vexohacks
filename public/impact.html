Part 2

How we got these numbers

Relay has no field data yet, so we can't measure its impact directly. We combine three things:

  • What published studies say happens today — how often each piece of information makes it into a handoff, how long crews spend writing reports.
  • What we can measure about Relay itself — scripted ambulance calls run through the real code, every expected fact checked.
  • Explicit assumptions for everything else — each with a low, most likely, and high value.

We run the arithmetic 10,000 times with different combinations of those values to get a range of outcomes rather than one number.

Step 1 — Measure Relay on scripted calls

Six realistic calls with synthetic patients: chest pain, a car crash, a stroke, an asthma attack, low blood sugar, and an elderly fall. 59 spoken lines total, including small talk and one correction. Each line was fed through the same code the product runs:

  1. OpenAI (gpt-6-luna) proposes an action for the line.
  2. Relay's engine checks the proposal against the current task state.
  3. The handoff is built from that state.

We then checked whether each of the 62 expected facts appeared in the handoff. Result: 62 of 62 captured, 59 of 59 lines classified correctly.

Step 2 — Handoff completeness formula

Goldberg 2017 gives the baseline rate b for each element. Relay can only add an element the crew would have left out, and only when all four of these hold:

with Relay = b + (1 − b) × capture × speech-to-text × said_aloud × adoption

Worked example — vital signs at most likely values:

0.57 + (1 − 0.57) × (0.964 × 0.93 × 0.80 × 0.80)
= 0.57 + 0.43 × 0.574
= 0.80   →  full vitals reach the hospital in 80% of handoffs instead of 57%

The 0.964 is the measured capture for vitals: (26 + 1) / (26 + 2), explained in Step 5.

Step 3 — Time and money per call

minutes saved   = report-writing minutes × share Relay saves × adoption
                = 17.2 × 0.25 × 0.80  =  3.44 min

crew time ($)   = minutes / 60 × crew-hour wage × overhead
                = 3.44 / 60 × $50.50 × 1.35  =  $3.91

claim cost ($)  = claims per transport × share from comm failures
                  × share of those that are handoffs × share a tool averts
                  × share of omissions Relay fills × cost per claim
                = (2 / 100,000) × 0.49 × 0.40 × 0.77 × 0.43 × $237,600 = $0.31

running cost    = API cost per call × adoption = $0.25 × 0.80  =  $0.20

net per call    = $3.91 + $0.31 − $0.20  =  $4.01
net per year    = $4.01 × 10,000 transports  ≈  $40,100

Step 5 — Monte Carlo (turning ranges into a range of outcomes)

Every input has a low, most likely, and high value. Each of the 10,000 runs picks one value per input:

  • Assumed and published inputs are drawn from a triangle distribution. Values near the most likely one come up more often.
  • Measured capture rates are drawn from Beta(hits + 1, misses + 1) — the standard way to express uncertainty about a success rate from a small test. A perfect 6 of 6 still allows the true rate to be lower; it centres around 87–91%, not 100%.

The random seed is fixed, so the same inputs always produce the same report. The sensitivity table (Part 1) moves one input to its low/high while holding all others at their most likely value.

Part 3

What the numbers mean

In plain terms

$40,000 a year, or about $4 per call, for a 10,000-transport agency. That is roughly two-thirds of one paramedic's median salary ($60,600). Scales to ~$100,000 at 25,000 transports.

  • The saving is almost all crew time, not legal risk. Of the $4 per call, ~$3.90 is report time saved and ~$0.30–0.50 is avoided claim cost. We would not pitch Relay as a liability tool.
  • About 550 crew-hours a year. With two people per crew, that is ~1,100 person-hours — roughly half a full-time position, or the capacity to cover ~4% more calls (~416).
  • Handoffs go from 54% to 72% complete. About 2.7 of 5 elements today; ~3.6 with Relay. Roughly one more element per patient.
  • Vitals and exam findings improve most. Full vitals: 57% → 80%. Crews already call vitals out to each other.
  • Overall assessment improves least (31% → 48%). Crews rarely say their overall impression aloud, and Relay can't record what isn't said.
  • Completeness is a per-handoff figure — it doesn't depend on agency size.

The most important caveat

The dollar figure rests mostly on one assumption: Relay saves 25% of report-writing time. No published study gives us this number. If Relay saved no report time at all, the net saving would fall to about $1,000 a year. If it saved 40%, the net saving would rise to about $64,000. The handoff-completeness benefit holds either way. The financial case needs a pilot that times reports with and without Relay.

  • Every one of the 10,000 runs showed a net saving. The lowest was $5,205.
  • 96.5% of runs exceeded $20,000 a year.
  • The simulation is stable: changing the random seed moves the median by only about ±$300.

This is not a classical statistical confidence interval. It shows how uncertainty in the inputs spreads to the outputs — only as good as the input ranges, and several of those are our judgment. The interval also doesn't include model structure assumptions, Relay misrecording a value, or real ambulance noise.

Confidence in the measured capture rate

62 of 62 is a strong result on a small test.

  • All 62 facts together: at 95% confidence, the true capture rate on this kind of input is at least about 94%.
  • One element on its own (e.g. the 6 scene facts): the 95% lower bound is only about 54%. That is why the model doesn't treat capture as 100%.

What would raise our confidence fastest

  • 01

    Time call reports with and without Relay on a few shifts. This replaces the biggest assumption and directly validates the financial case.

  • 02

    Re-run the capture test on recorded band audio from a moving ambulance. Replaces the speech-to-text assumption and tests real noise, crosstalk, and accents.

  • 03

    Count what crews actually say aloud during real calls. Replaces the "said aloud" family of assumptions — currently the second-largest source of uncertainty.

  • 04

    Log actual API spend per call. Replaces the running-cost assumption. Each of these inputs is already in the model, so new data drops straight in and the report regenerates with npm run impact.
